Peerfall

Learn FRRouting on a real network, in your browser.

Peerfall is a free, open-source browser game where you build networks, diagnose outages and verify recovery with FRRouting (FRR). The network is real: FRR 10.6.1 on a Linux kernel running in an emulated PC inside your browser, with one network namespace per router and FRR's own daemons in each. You type into the real vtysh, and the routes are in the real Linux forwarding table. There is nothing to install and no server: your progress stays in your browser.

Who it's for

Anyone who wants to learn how routers work by running one, and network engineers who want to get hands-on with FRR. You start knowing nothing: one router, a terminal, and a guide who explains each command before you run it and what came back after. Objectives tick by themselves as the network changes, there are no timers, and hints come in three steps.

The journey, in eleven stages

  1. First router: read interfaces and routes, start FRR, use vtysh, and save a configuration. Playable now.
  2. Follow a packet: address interfaces, follow connected and static routes, and keep a backup route waiting. Playable now.
  3. Build an underlay: form OSPF and IS-IS adjacencies, read the link-state database, and predict the path from costs.
  4. Recover from failure: measure an outage, detect it with BFD, and restart a daemon without losing traffic. Playable now.
  5. The other interior protocols: RIP, EIGRP, Babel, OpenFabric and NHRP, and when each one fits.
  6. Exchange routes: iBGP and eBGP sessions, best-path selection, route reflectors and address families.
  7. Control what is advertised: prefix lists, communities, route maps, RPKI and policy routing.
  8. Isolate and connect tenants: VRFs, route leaking, L3VPN, and EVPN over VXLAN.
  9. Label and steer: MPLS with LDP, Segment Routing, SRv6 and engineered paths.
  10. Deliver multicast: PIM, IGMP and MLD, rendezvous points and MSDP.
  11. Operate a network: diagnose across protocol state and forwarding, read debugs, automate with JSON, and roll back.

Stages 1, 2 and 4 have missions today; the rest are planned.

Before you play

  • Play on a desktop browser with a window at least 1024 pixels wide.
  • The first visit downloads about 0.3 MB before the title screen is usable and about 15 MB until the first mission's terminal is ready, most of it the Linux kernel. Once a network is up, the rest of the machine (about 7 MB) is cached, and the game then plays offline.
